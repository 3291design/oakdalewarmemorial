Frederick G. Goodman
Died: 25 July 1917
Age: 33 years old
Rank: Private
Regtl. No.: 19476
Regiment: South Wales Borderers
Medals: 1914-1915 Star, Victory, British War

Frederick George Goodman, was a coal miner from Brithdir, South Wales, who left the collieries to enlist in the war effort in April 1915. Joining the South Wales Borderers under service number 19476, his military journey quickened when he arrived on the Western Front in September 1915. This crucial timeline indicates he was sent out as a vital reinforcement for the 1st Battalion, South Wales Borderers, right as they were thrown into the devastating and bloody fighting of the Battle of Loos. From that baptism of fire onward, Goodman endured years in the trenches, with his battalion going on to face some of the most harrowing campaigns of the war, including the Somme and Passchendaele.
Throughout the war, Goodman suffered physical impairments due to the harsh reality of service on the Western Front. By the time the Armistice was signed, his injuries meant he was no longer fit for duty. In January 1919, at the age of 33, he was honourably discharged as surplus to military requirements due to his medical impairments. Upon returning to civilian life, he was awarded the Silver War Badge, a lapel pin worn on civilian clothes to signify to the public that he had served his country bravely and protecting him from being mistaken for a draft evader.
Tragically, the physical toll of his wartime service followed him into the post-war era. Like many veterans whose lungs were compromised by poison gas or whose bodies carried deep shrapnel wounds, his health ultimately collapsed under the strain, and it is understood that he later succumbed to the long-term effects of his injuries. Because he passed away after his official discharge, he is locally remembered rather than carrying a standard military grave, with his name permanently honored on the Oakdale War Memorial alongside his fallen neighbours.
