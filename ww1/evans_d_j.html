David John Evans enlisted in the Royal Garrison Artillery (RGA) responsible for the army's heaviest ordnance, operating massive howitzers and siege guns designed to destroy fortified positions and clear the way for infantry advances. Evans was assigned to the 380th Siege Battery, which was deployed to the Middle Eastern theatre as part of the heavy artillery group supporting the XXI Corps of the Egyptian Expeditionary Force.
Ramleh War Cemetery, Israel.
Photo: CWGC Archive
In late 1917, Gunner Evans and his battery were thrust against the forces of the Ottoman Empire. Following the capture of Beersheba, the Expeditionary Force turned its sights onto the heavily defended coastal stronghold of Gaza. The heavy howitzers of the 380th Siege Battery eventually shattered the Turkish lines, allowing Allied forces to capture Gaza in early November. From there, Evans's corps pushed northward along the Mediterranean coast, forcing the Ottoman retreat and ultimately facilitating the Allied entry into Jerusalem.
During the intense fighting that accompanied this rapid northern advance, Gunner Evans was mortally wounded and evacuated to a field hospital behind the lines where, on 19 December 1917, he succumbed to his wounds. Records indicate he was approximately 24 years old at the time of his passing, and he was noted as the eldest son of Henry and Mary Evans of nearby Pontypridd.
Gunner Evans was laid to rest in the Ramleh War Cemetery, now modern-day Israel—a central burial site chosen for soldiers who died in the medical facilities at Ramleh and Lydda during the push toward Jerusalem.
