Richard Henry Harvey was a Glamorgan-born coal miner who lived in Oakdale with his wife, Alice, and their young daughter. The son of Charles Harvey, Richard enlisted for military service in the 4th Battalion, South Wales Borderers — a battle-hardened unit that had already survived the horrors of the Gallipoli campaign in 1915 before being reassigned to the harsh, disease-ridden landscapes of the southern Mesopotamian theatre (modern-day Iraq) in early 1916. There, they were attached to the 40th Brigade, 13th (Western) Division, tasked with breaking through a stubborn Ottoman blockade to rescue a starved British garrison trapped at Kut-al-Amara.
The climax of Private Harvey's service came on the morning of 22 April 1916 during the catastrophic Third Battle of Sannaiyat. The brigade was ordered to launch a desperate, frontal assault against heavily fortified Turkish positions. Nature, however, had turned the battlefield into a death trap; seasonal flooding from the Tigris River had transformed the terrain into a knee-deep quagmire of thick mud. With absolutely no natural cover, the men of the 4th Battalion advanced valiantly through the swamp, only to be cut down en masse by a devastating curtain of Ottoman machine-gun and artillery fire. The assault was completely repulsed with staggering casualties, marking the final failed attempt to save the garrison at Kut.
Tragically, 30-year-old Private Richard Henry Harvey (19883) was killed in action during the chaotic advance. Because the waterlogged, fiercely contested battlefield made the recovery and identification of the fallen impossible, he has no known grave. Instead, his name is permanently chiseled into Panels 16 and 62 of the Basra Memorial in Iraq, which honors more than 40,000 Commonwealth soldiers who died in the region with no identifiable resting place. Though his name was omitted from the physical Oakdale War Memorial, his memory was preserved by his widow, Alice—who later relocated to Pengam Road, Ystrad Mynach—and he remains locally commemorated on the neighboring Ystrad Mynach Cenotaph and Hengoed Roll of Honour.
Additional information taken from the Army Registers of Soldiers' Effects, 1901-1929: Richard's widow Alice received pay of £2 17s and a gratuity of £3. Omission Historical Note: *His name does not appear on the physical Oakdale Memorial.
