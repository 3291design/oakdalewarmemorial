Samuel Fisher

Samuel Fisher was a coal miner from Blackwood, South Wales, the son of the late Alfred and Sarah Fisher. He enlisted in the South Wales Borderers, receiving the service number 3/13341. The "3/" prefix indicates he initially joined the 3rd (Reserve) Battalion, a home-based unit responsible for training and processing recruits. However, his deployment details reveal a front-line reality: he arrived in the Balkans in June 1915, a timeline that perfectly aligns with the 4th Battalion, South Wales Borderers, which sailed that month to join the grueling and bloody Gallipoli Campaign against Ottoman forces.

Private Fisher was wounded in action and returned home to recuperate under the care of his aunt, Mrs. M. Crew, at her residence on High Street, Blackwood. Though his physical wounds may have begun to heal, his time in the military was drawing to a close due to a shift in British wartime policy. He was officially transferred to the Class W Reserve, a special category introduced in June 1916 for soldiers whose specialized skills were deemed far more valuable to the home front's civilian infrastructure than to active combat operations.
As an experienced miner, Fisher was sent back into the South Wales valleys to work in the coals mines. Tragically, his return to civil employment was short-lived. The severe respiratory strain of working deep underground, coupled with a constitution permanently weakened by his combat injuries and the harsh environment of the Mediterranean, ultimately proved fatal. On 25 July 1917, at the age of 44, Private Fisher succumbed to pneumonia.
Because his death was directly linked to the illness and wounds suffered during his time in uniform, he was recognized as a casualty of the Great War. Today, he rests in the Penmaen Chapelyard near Blackwood beneath a permanent Commonwealth War Graves Commission headstone, and his sacrifice remains locally honored with his name inscribed upon the Oakdale War Memorial.
