John T. Williams was an elementary school teacher from Oakdale who answered the call to serve, enlisting as a Private with the 11th Battalion South Wales Borderers.
Before his military service, Williams dedicated his life to shaping the minds of local children, a role that stood in stark contrast to the violence of the Western Front. His transition from a peaceful classroom to the mud of the Givenchy sector reflects the profound sacrifice made by a generation of educators. Today, his name is remembered not only on his headstone in France but also in the quiet legacy of the community he left behind in Oakdale.
In December 1915, he deployed to the Western Front where he sustained severe injuries during the brutal reality of static combat operations in the Givenchy trenches.
Private J T Williams (21985) succumbed to his wounds on 25 May 1916, at the age of 29. He was laid to rest and is permanently commemorated in the Merville Communal Cemetery, France.
Additional information taken from the Army Registers of Soldiers' Effects, 1901-1929: Following his death, John's mother received his remaining back-pay and a war gratuity.
