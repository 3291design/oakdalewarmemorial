Ivor James Nelmes
Died: 24 December 1918
Age: 20 years old
Rank: Seaman, Second Class
Service No.: J26377
Regiment: Royal Navy, H.M.S. Vivid I
Medals: 1914-1915 Star, Victory, British War
Royal Navy Service View Timeline
Ivor James Nelmes, was born on 28 May 1898, the son of Martha Nelmes, of 6, Richmond Terrace, Tredegar. He was adopted by John and Elizabeth Higgs, of 2, Markham Crescent, Oakdale. Ivor worked as a Collier’s Boy prior to enlisting into the Royal Navy on his sixteenth birthday and was soon posted to HMS Impregnable, as a Boy, second class, in August 1913. When the war erupted in August of 1914, he was sationed aboard the HMS Hawke.
In September 1915 ,Ivor was then posted to HMS Vivid I before soon moving to the HMS Minerva. In December of 1915, Ivor received 90 days detention for improper behaviour and upon his release was posted to the HMS Implacable. After serving three months, he was again re-posted back to HMS Vivid. Later that year, he was moved the Town-class Light Cruiser HMS Dublin, which was attached to the 2nd Light Cruiser Squadron of the Grand Fleet. During the Battle of Jutland, the Dublin was hit 13 times by German light cruisers and had to undergo extensive repairs spending several months back at dock.
It was during this time that was finally re-posted back to HMS Vivid, but unfortuantely, in July 1917 he became unwell and on 22 August 1917 he was discharged from the Royal Navy. Having been found to have contracted pulmonary tuberculosis, he returned home to his step-parents in Oakdale to recuperate.
Able Seaman Nelmes (J26377) served continuously from September 1914 through July 1917 aboard several strategic warships until he was invalided out of active service suffering from advanced tuberculosis. Tragically, on 24 December 1918, Ivor succumed to tuberculosis, aged 20.
Commemoration Details: For many years, the exact resting place for Ivor was unverified, leading him to be remembered via the United Kingdom Book of Remembrance. Following research by the West Wales War Memorial Project, his grave was located at St. Sannan’s Churchyard, allowing him to be formally submitted and accepted for official Commonwealth War Graves Commission (CWGC) commemoration and headstone marking (details pending).
