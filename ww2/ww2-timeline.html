1939
01 September
Invasion of Poland
German forces cross the Polish border at dawn, triggering British and French ultimatums and plunging Europe into global conflict.
03 September
Britain Declares War on Germany
Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain announces to the nation on 3 September 1939, that Britain was at war with Germany, following the German invasion of Poland and the expiration of the British ultimatum.
September 1939
Battle of the Atlantic
German U-boats began targeting Allied merchant shipping lanes, opening the longest continuous military campaign of the war.

Coal pits, such as the Oakdale Colliery and ports along the Bristol Channel became vital lifelines for the wartime economy.
1940
10 May
Winston Churchill becomes Prime Minister on the same day Germany launches its Blitzkrieg invasion of France, Belgium, and the Netherlands.
26 May – 04 Jun. 1940
Dunkirk Evacuation
Cut off by rapid German armored thrusts to the English Channel, over 338,000 British and Allied troops are rescued from the beaches and harbour of Dunkirk during Operation Dynamo by Royal Navy warships and a flotilla of civilian “Little Ships.”
Jul. – Oct. 1940
Battle of Britain & The Blitz
RAF Fighter Command repulses the Luftwaffe’s relentless air attacks over the United Kingdom and forestalls a German land invasion.
By September, daytime dogfights shift to “The Blitz”—relentless German night bombing raids against British cities, ports, and industrial centres.

Jan. – Feb. 1941
The Blitz Comes to South Wales
Targeting the coal-exporting docks, railways, and oil refineries of the Bristol Channel, the Luftwaffe unleashed devastating night raids across South Wales—bringing the front line directly to the doorstep of the Sirhowy Valley.
02 January 1941
Cardiff Blitz
✪ German bombers strike Cardiff!
Cardiff Blitz
Over 100 bombers attacked the city in a devastating ten-hour night raid. 165 civilians were killed and Llandaff Cathedral was severely damaged.

The glow of Cardiff burning was visible from the ridges above Sirhowy Valley.
19 – 21 Feb. 1941
Swansea Blitz
✪ Swansea bombed for three nights!
Swansea Blitz
Luftwaffe bombers dropped 30,000 incendiaries and 800 high-explosive bombs over three nights, killing 230 people and levelling the town centre.

Targeted for its vital shipping docks, oil refinery, and coal exports, Swansea endured the most concentrated air raid over Wales during the war.

Pioneer Corps and defense logistics teams were vital for clearing bomb-damage in the UK.

Apr. – May 1941
Battle of Greece & Crete
British and Commonwealth expeditionary troops deployed from North Africa fight desperate mountain rearguard actions against invading German forces across mainland Greece and Crete.
22 June
Operation Barbarossa
Germany invades the Soviet Union along a 1,800-mile front, opening the vast Eastern Front.
20 September

07 – 08 Dec. 1941
War Erupts in the Far East
Japanese forces attack the US at Pearl Harbor while also invading British Malaya, Hong Kong, and the Philippines.
Consequently, Britain and the United States declare war on Japan, transforming the conflict into a truly global war.
1942
15 February 1942
Fall of Singapore
Following a rapid Japanese offensive, the British garrison at Singapore surrenders. Over 80,000 Allied troops become prisoners of war—many destined for the brutal Burma-Siam "Death" Railway.
27 February 1942
Battle of the Java Sea
Allied warships of the American-British-Dutch-Australian strike force clash with the Imperial Japanese Navy in the Dutch East Indies. Fighting through the night without air cover, the Royal Navy destroyer HMS Jupiter strikes a mine and sinks.
27 February
HMS Jupiter sank in the Java Sea 27 February 1942. 97 of her crew were captured by the Japanese, while 84 were dead or missing.

May – Nov. 1942
Escalation of the Air Offensive
RAF Bomber Command launches Operation Millennium—its first “1,000-bomber raid” against Cologne. As heavy four-engine Lancaster and Halifax bombers enter squadron service, night-time strategic raids intensify.
25 August
The Avro Lancaster was the main aircraft used during night-time bombing campaigns over Europe.

Oct. – Nov. 1942
El Alamein & North Africa
General Montgomery’s Eighth Army breaks Axis lines at the Second Battle of El Alamein in Egypt, while Anglo-American forces land in French North Africa (Operation Torch), turning the tide in the Mediterranean theatre.
1943
Jul. – Sep. 1943
Allied Invasion of Italy
Following victory in North Africa and the capture of Sicily, Allied armies land on the Italian mainland at Salerno in September 1943, beginning a grueling northward advance against entrenched German lines.

May – Nov. 1943
Continued Air Offensive Europe
In May 1943, No. 617 Squadron successfully executes the daring and legendary “Dambusters” raid against Germany’s Ruhr dams.
Throughout 1943, RAF Bomber Command wages the Battle of the Ruhr and launches Operation Gomorrah against Hamburg as radar-guided Pathfinder squadrons lead deep night-time strikes into Germany.

Jan. – May 1944
Anzio Beachhead & Defensive Struggles
On 22 January 1944, Allied forces launch Operation Shingle, landing at Anzio and Nettuno in a bold move to outflank the German Winter Line.
Contained within a narrow coastal perimeter, Allied troops endure four months of relentless German artillery fire, Luftwaffe raids, and fierce armoured counter-attacks.

Mediterranean, Egypt & Greece
Across the Mediterranean and Middle East, Royal Navy warships guard vital Malta and Suez convoys while RAF squadrons and ground forces sustain Allied operations from Cairo to Greece.

17 Jan. – 18 May
Breakout from Monte Cassino
Allied infantry divisions launch four major assaults against the mountain stronghold of Monte Cassino anchoring the German Gustav Line.
In May 1944, Operation Diadem finally shatters the Gustav Line, linking up with the Anzio beachhead and opening the road to Rome.

06 June 1944
D-Day: Normandy Landings
Allied forces launch Operation Overlord, the largest amphibious invasion in history – landing across five Normandy beaches on a single day.
British, American, and Canadian divisions breach the Atlantic Wall and fight through the French bocage to liberate Western Europe.
Summer 1944
Turning Point in Burma: Imphal & Kohima
British and Indian Fourteenth Army troops halt the Japanese invasion of India in brutal hand-to-hand fighting.
Supported by continuous RAF air supply drops, Allied forces break the siege and begin the decisive overland drive to liberate Burma.
06 December

16 Dec. 1944 – Jan. 1945
Battle of the Bulge
Germany launches a massive winter offensive through the forested Ardennes of Belgium and Luxembourg. Allied forces on the Meuse halt the armoured thrust, exhausting Germany’s final reserves on the Western Front.
1945

Crossing the Rhine
Allied armies breach the Rhine River (Operation Plunder) and advance into the industrial heartland of Germany.
08 May 1945
